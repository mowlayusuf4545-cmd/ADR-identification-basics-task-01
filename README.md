# ADR Identification Basics – Pharmacovigilance Intern Task

## Case Scenario

**Patient:** 45-year-old female  
**Medical history:** Hypertension  
**Regular medication:** Amlodipine 5 mg once daily  
**New medication:** Amoxicillin-clavulanate 625 mg three times daily for a respiratory tract infection

### Clinical Timeline

- **Day 1:** Amoxicillin-clavulanate was started.
- **Day 4:** The patient developed a generalized itchy skin rash with red maculopapular lesions over the trunk and upper limbs.
- She had no new food, cosmetics, or other medicines during this period.
- She was afebrile and had no breathing difficulty, facial swelling, hypotension, or mucosal involvement.
- **Day 4:** The suspected antibiotic was discontinued after medical review.
- **Day 7:** The rash and itching substantially improved after withdrawal and symptomatic treatment.
- No rechallenge was performed because intentional rechallenge was not clinically necessary.

## ADR Identification

**Suspected ADR:** Generalized maculopapular skin rash with pruritus.

**Suspected drug:** Amoxicillin-clavulanate.

**ADR type:** A (augmented/predictable pharmacological reaction) is less appropriate here than a hypersensitivity-type reaction; clinically, this presentation is consistent with a delayed cutaneous drug hypersensitivity reaction.

## Causality Assessment – WHO-UMC

The relationship is assessed as **Probable/Likely** because:

1. There is a reasonable temporal relationship between starting amoxicillin-clavulanate and development of the rash.
2. The reaction is a recognized type of adverse reaction associated with beta-lactam antibiotics.
3. The patient improved after withdrawal of the suspected drug (positive dechallenge).
4. No stronger alternative cause was identified from the available history.
5. Rechallenge was not performed, which is not required for a probable/likely assessment.

The WHO-UMC system used by the Pharmacovigilance Programme of India considers temporal relationship, alternative explanations, and response to withdrawal among the important elements of causality assessment.

## Severity and Seriousness

**Severity:** Mild to moderate, based on the information provided.

**Seriousness:** Non-serious because there was no hospitalization, death, life-threatening condition, disability, congenital anomaly, or other serious outcome described.

## Management

- Stop the suspected medicine after clinical assessment.
- Provide appropriate symptomatic treatment for itching/rash as prescribed.
- Monitor for progression to severe cutaneous or systemic hypersensitivity.
- Document the suspected antibiotic reaction clearly in the patient's medical record.
- Counsel the patient to inform healthcare professionals about the suspected reaction before receiving the same or related medicine in the future.
- Report the suspected ADR through the appropriate pharmacovigilance reporting system when applicable.

## Conclusion

The patient developed a generalized pruritic maculopapular rash after initiation of amoxicillin-clavulanate. The timing, clinical presentation, improvement after withdrawal, and absence of a stronger alternative explanation support a **probable/likely suspected ADR**. The case should be documented and reported according to local pharmacovigilance procedures.
